## 3. How the boards were routed

Freerouting 2.4.1 was tried first on both boards and did not finish: Board B stopped at 27 unrouted connections after
27 passes; Board A needed 8-9 minutes per pass, reported 1277 violations at pass 12 and threw a NullPointerException in
its search tree. The boards are routed by the scripts in `gen/` instead, in this order (D32):

1. **Power copper** (`route_power.py`): pours over every switching stage's switch node and current loop (taken from the
   TI layout examples the placement followed), the battery path and the CM5 input as rectangles, VSYS on L4 under the
   power section, via arrays between pours of the same net, then wide trunks for the long supply runs (VSYS to the LTE
   regulator and the amplifiers, USB-C input to the charger, 5 V to the USB ports, ...), narrowing to the pin width in
   the last 1.3 mm at fine-pitch pins (D40).
2. **Differential pairs** (`route_pairs.py`): each pair routed as one coupled path at its impedance geometry, with
   staggered via fan-outs at the 0.4 mm CM5 connectors, straight escapes at the camera connectors (D38), and the P/N
   swap some pairs need done at a layer change with staggered vias (both traces cross over on different layers, equal
   length, no detour; D43).
   - A layer change of the coupled section that falls on a corner (where one trace would pass the other's via) is
     moved back onto the straight incoming run where there is room; the CSI-A lanes keep 0.7 mm from their camera
     connector before changing layer (D66).
   - The CSI-A lanes' long fan-out strips east of the CM5 connector are reserved first (D70).
   - Every finished pair is checked exactly, with KiCad's own shapes, against the 0.09 mm pair rule and the 0.2 mm
     via-hole rule, and is routed again if it fails (D54).
   - Pairs that fail are routed again first in the next round (6 rounds, the best is kept). A pair still open after
     that is routed as two separate traces at its impedance width (D71; section 7 names it).
3. **Length tuning** (`route_tune.py`): first every pair's copper that is not on its pad-to-pad path is removed
   (a hairpin left where the P/N order is untwisted, a track folded back over itself: copper the signal does not
   use, which would count as length; D79); then intra-pair skew, with U-shaped bumps on the shorter trace (D39), then
   the MIPI lanes of each link matched to the longest lane with whole-pair accordions, the clock lane among them;
   where a data lane has no room to follow, the clock lane goes to the middle of its data lanes instead (D80).
   - Lengths are counted the way KiCad's DRC counts them (D83), so the tuning, the length report and KiCad's skew
     check agree.
   - A bump may not touch other copper of its own net (the signal would short past it); a pair is pruned again
     after it is tuned and tuned again if that removed anything (D83).
4. **GND fan-out** (`fanout.py`): every SMD GND pad gets its own via (via-in-pad on Board A where it fits, else a short
   stub), D36.
5. **The microSD bus** (D75), with the grid router of step 6 on its own, before the other signals, and locked: as a
   long 50 ohm bus across the power section it was what the full negotiation left open in most runs.
6. **Everything else** (`groute.py` + `groute.c`): a grid router with negotiated congestion (PathFinder): 0.05 mm
   cells, every existing copper item rasterised with its exact KiCad outline, inflated by its clearance plus 0.02 mm
   (paths run on cell centres, so that covers the rounding), A* in C over layers, cells and directions, 45-degree turns
   cheap, 90-degree dear. All open connections are routed, then re-routed with growing costs wherever two nets share
   space, until none do.
   - What is open comes from KiCad: its DRC report (zones filled), or pcbnew's connectivity when that report reaches
     its 499-item limit (D57).
   - Board A: GND pins without a fan-out via are routed in the same negotiation as the signals, to the nearest GND via.
   - Impedance-class nets keep their width end to end (D53). Other wide nets (power) narrow to the default width within
     1 mm of their pins and keep their own clearance there.
   - Every via keeps the hole-to-hole distance from every hole, its own net's included (D46).
   - What the board-wide negotiation cannot settle is routed again in a window around it on a 0.025 mm grid, after
     the router's own copper inside the window is taken up (D61).
   - What is still open after that gets rip-up and re-route, one connection at a time: a trial route through the
     router's own copper (at a cost) shows which router tracks and vias are in the way; they are taken up and routed
     again together with the open connection. The change is kept only if fewer connections are open afterwards, or
     as many but much shorter ones, which are then closed in small windows (D74, D78).
7. **GND pours** on the outer layers (Board A: tied to the L2 / L5 planes by the fan-out vias and a 2.5 mm stitching
   grid; Board B: both sides, 3 mm stitching grid, GND also routed as a net so it does not depend on the pours), then a
   last router pass for what the pours left apart.
8. **Clean-up, driven by KiCad's DRC**: dead-end tracks and vias are removed (they carry nothing); pads where the pour
   cannot attach two thermal spokes get a solid connection instead; the last clearance errors on router copper are
   re-routed in small windows. None of it may leave more connections open than before, and what is open after it gets
   one more rip-up pass (D77). A piece of a GND pour cut off together with the pads it touches is found from the
   fill and joined like any other open connection (D78).
   Last, loops and fold-backs on the nets without a pour are removed the same way as in step 3 (D79).

Steps 1-5 keep a 0.6 mm escape lane free in front of every signal pin of the fine-pitch parts (D42). Before the
pairs, Board A's Wi-Fi antenna lines are routed as straight 50 ohm tracks (D62), and the camera connectors' boxed-in
front-row GND pins get a stub and via (D60).

## 6. Length matching

Targets and their sources (downloaded or read 2026-10-20; `research/length_targets.md`):

| What | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|

from its centre, and each via's height in the stack-up between the layers its tracks use. Times use 7.0 ps/mm
(sqrt(4.4) / c for the inner-layer prepreg, Er 4.4); the outer layers are faster, so the times are upper bounds.
